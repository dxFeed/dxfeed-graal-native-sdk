Detailed Description

The events of subscriptions received on the application's own thread, without callbacks.

The application polls an event queue (dxfg_v4_EventQueue_poll(), dxfg_v4_EventQueue_pollTagged()) on a thread of its own: the call waits for events and returns them as a list. No thread of the SDK calls the application, which suits the languages that cannot take a call from a foreign thread.

A queue has the subscriptions attached to it as its sources:

  • dxfg_v4_EventQueue_new() creates a queue bound to one subscription: the queue closes when the subscription closes, and nothing else can be attached to it. Its events have the tag 0.
  • dxfg_v4_EventQueue_create() creates a shared queue, and dxfg_v4_EventQueue_attach() attaches subscriptions to it, each with a tag of the application's choice (tags need not be unique). dxfg_v4_EventQueue_pollTagged() returns each event with the tag of its subscription, so one thread serves many subscriptions. A subscription leaves the queue only by being closed: then the queue stays open, a poll in progress goes on, the events the subscription delivered before stay in the queue, and its later events are discarded and counted as dropped.

An attached subscription gets an executor of the queue (see dxfg_v4_DXFeedSubscription_setExecutor()): the SDK retrieves the events from its buffers only when a poll runs, on the polling thread. So the queue keeps no copy of the events, and until the next poll, as with a listener that is slow to return:

  • The lasting events (e.g. Quote, Trade, Summary, Profile) are conflated: the SDK keeps the last event of each symbol, and none is counted as dropped.
  • The other events (e.g. TimeAndSale) wait in a buffer of the subscription, up to 1000000 events by default. Beyond it, the oldest ones are dropped and counted (dxfg_v4_EventQueue_getDroppedEvents()). The limit is the system property com.devexperts.qd.impl.matrix.Agent.MaxBufferSize, set with dxfg_v4_system_set_property() before the first endpoint of the isolate is created; it applies to all the subscriptions and connections of the isolate.
  • An endpoint with the role DXFG_ENDPOINT_ROLE_STREAM_FEED drops nothing: once the buffer of a subscription is full, the endpoint stops receiving data, also for its other subscriptions, until the queue is polled.

The models and the instrument profile collector run on the queue too: give them the executor of a shared queue (dxfg_v4_EventQueue_getExecutor()), e.g. with dxfg_v4_IndexedTxModel_Builder_withExecutor(), dxfg_v4_IndexedEventModel_setExecutor() or dxfg_v4_InstrumentProfileCollector_setExecutor(). Their listeners are then called by the polls, on the polling thread, between the events of the subscriptions. A poll that ran their work and has no events returns DXFG_EVENT_QUEUE_NO_EVENTS at once, so DXFG_EVENT_QUEUE_NO_EVENTS also means that the listeners of the models may have been called. Their own data is not in the list: it reaches their listeners. Limits:

  • The calls of a model that notify on the calling thread (setSymbol, clear, dxfg_v4_OrderBookModel_setFilter()) call its listener there, not in a poll.
  • The work posted before the executor was set runs on the previous executor.
  • The events that a model drops are not counted by dxfg_v4_EventQueue_getDroppedEvents().
  • The collector posts its work once per update listener and update: poll a queue with a collector regularly.
  • A subscription whose executor is the executor of a queue (set with dxfg_v4_DXFeedSubscription_setExecutor(), or inherited from an endpoint built with dxfg_v4_DXEndpoint_Builder_withExecutor()) is not attached: its events reach its listeners, not the list. dxfg_v4_DXFeed_detachSubscriptionAndClear() of it and dxfg_v4_DXEndpoint_closeAndAwaitTermination() of its endpoint wait until a poll runs its pending work or the queue is closed, so do not call them on the polling thread.

Executors:

  • The executor of an attached subscription (dxfg_v4_DXFeedSubscription_getExecutor()) belongs to it: it cannot be changed, and the functions that accept an executor (the setters of the subscriptions, the controllers, the models and the collector, the endpoint builder, the asynchronous promise handlers) refuse it with IllegalStateException.
  • The executor of a shared queue (dxfg_v4_EventQueue_getExecutor()) is accepted by all of them, except dxfg_v4_ExecutorBaseOnConcurrentLinkedQueue_processAllPendingTasks(), which fails with IllegalArgumentException. A subscription that has it as its executor may change it later; it cannot be attached, since it has an executor.

Rules:

  • Do not set an executor on a subscription to be attached: a subscription with an executor or a queue cannot be attached (IllegalStateException). Symbols may be added before or after, but as for any listener in QD, a subscription attached to a feed that already has symbols cannot be attached (IllegalStateException): attach it before the symbols are added, or before the subscription is attached to the feed. For QD a queue is an attachment too: on a subscription that has symbols, add the event listeners before the queue, a listener added after it is refused (IllegalStateException).
  • One consumer: the polls of a queue are serialized, a poll waits for the one in progress on another thread. Poll a queue from one thread at a time, so that a snapshot of indexed or time-series events is not split.
  • The event listeners added to an attached subscription are called by the poll, on the polling thread, in the order of the SDK, and on the thread that closes the queue or the subscription, or detaches the subscription. Do not poll the queue from such a listener or from a listener of a model on the queue: the poll fails. A listener that fails makes the poll fail too, and the events it has retrieved, of every tag, are dropped and counted. To receive the same events by a listener and by a queue, use two subscriptions: the subscriptions of a feed, with or without a queue, do not affect each other.
  • A list returned by a poll is the caller's: it does not depend on the queue, the subscriptions or the endpoint, and any thread attached to the isolate may release it (dxfg_v4_event_type_list_release(), dxfg_v4_tagged_event_list_release()).
  • Keep the handle of a shared queue, or the handle of its executor, until the models and the collector on the queue are closed: the release of the last of them closes the queue.

A poll ends when events arrive, when the work of the models has run, when the timeout elapses, when the queue is woken up (dxfg_v4_EventQueue_wakeUp()) and when the queue is closed. The queue is closed by dxfg_v4_EventQueue_close(), by the release of the last handle of the queue and of its executor, by the close of the subscription of a bound queue, by dxfg_v4_NativeListener_deactivateAll() and by dxfg_v4_system_shutdown(); the last two also close the queues created after them. A closed queue runs the work posted to it at once, on the posting thread, without calling any listener of the application: from then on the queue, its models and its collector call nothing back, while their objects stay usable until the application closes them. The events of a closed queue are discarded and counted as dropped. Closing an endpoint does not close a queue and does not end a poll: an application that closes the endpoints itself should poll with a finite timeout. While an open queue holds events that no poll has returned, dxfg_v4_DXEndpoint_closeAndAwaitTermination() of the endpoint of an attached subscription waits until the queue is polled or closed. dxfg_v4_DXFeed_detachSubscriptionAndClear() of an attached subscription does not wait for a poll: the events that are delivered while it runs arrive in the poll in progress or the next one, and the work of the other subscriptions and of the models stays in the queue.

Ready signal (experimental): a host with an event loop gives the queue an OS object of its own, and the SDK makes the object ready whenever the queue has work. The loop waits for the object and polls with the timeout 0 on its own thread, so the host keeps no thread that waits in a poll (dxfg_v4_EventQueue_setReadySignal(); the feature DXFG_V4_FEATURE_EVENT_QUEUE_READY_SIGNAL). The object is a file descriptor on Linux, macOS and iOS (an eventfd, or the write end of a pipe) and an event on Windows. The contract of the host:

  • One object per queue. The host creates it, owns it and closes it: the SDK never creates, duplicates or closes it. Once the removal of the signal or the close of the queue has returned, or a poll has returned DXFG_EVENT_QUEUE_CLOSED, the SDK does not write to the object and never will, whichever thread removes the signal or closes the queue.
  • Work is the events of a subscription, the work of a model or of the collector, a wake-up and the close. The queue signals when it gets work while it is armed, and the signal disarms it; each poll arms it again, and a poll that leaves work behind (for example at its limit) signals again. So no work is left without a signal. A signal without work is possible, and its poll returns DXFG_EVENT_QUEUE_NO_EVENTS.
  • Reset the object before the poll, so that a signal sent during the poll is not lost: read the 8 bytes of an eventfd once, read a pipe until EAGAIN; an auto-reset Windows event resets itself, a manual-reset one needs ResetEvent(). After the object becomes ready, reset it and make one poll with the timeout 0.
  • A wake-up (dxfg_v4_EventQueue_wakeUp()) signals as work does. But a wake-up that an overlapping poll consumes may write nothing: a host that wakes the loop to recheck its own state (a stop flag) rechecks it after the poll too.
  • To stop, close the queue (dxfg_v4_EventQueue_close(), from any thread). The loop sees DXFG_EVENT_QUEUE_CLOSED, removes the object from its wait and then closes it, so the loop, not the thread that closed the queue, closes the object. Closing it earlier makes the SDK write to a closed or reused descriptor.
  • The descriptor must be non-blocking (a full blocking pipe would stall the thread that writes) and open for writing; the setting is refused otherwise. A full pipe and a saturated eventfd are already readable, so the SDK counts a write that would block as done. A write that fails otherwise (EBADF, EPIPE), or a failed SetEvent(), is logged once per signal, and the next work tries again.
  • SIGPIPE: a write to a pipe whose read end is closed fails with EPIPE. On Linux and macOS the isolate keeps the process alive for it (unless the application restores the default action of SIGPIPE); iOS has no such handler, so keep the read end of the pipe open until the signal is removed.
  • The asyncio proactor loop and libuv on Windows cannot wait for a foreign event: there the host keeps a reader thread, which polls with a timeout and passes the batches to the loop.

An epoll loop on Linux:

int fd = eventfd(0, EFD_NONBLOCK | EFD_CLOEXEC);
dxfg_v4_EventQueue_setReadySignal(thread, queue, DXFG_V4_READY_SIGNAL_FD, fd);
int epfd = epoll_create1(EPOLL_CLOEXEC);
struct epoll_event ev = { .events = EPOLLIN, .data.fd = fd };
epoll_ctl(epfd, EPOLL_CTL_ADD, fd, &ev);
for (bool running = true; running;) {
struct epoll_event ready;
if (epoll_wait(epfd, &ready, 1, -1) > 0 && ready.data.fd == fd) {
uint64_t counter;
read(fd, &counter, sizeof counter); // reset before the poll
dxfg_v4_event_type_list *events = NULL;
int32_t result = dxfg_v4_EventQueue_poll(thread, queue, 0, 0, &events);
if (result == DXFG_EXECUTE_SUCCESSFULLY) {
// handle the events, then dxfg_v4_event_type_list_release(thread, events);
} else if (result == DXFG_EVENT_QUEUE_CLOSED) {
running = false;
} // DXFG_EVENT_QUEUE_NO_EVENTS: wait again
}
}
epoll_ctl(epfd, EPOLL_CTL_DEL, fd, NULL);
close(fd); // the loop closes the object

Python asyncio on Linux and macOS: make the object (os.eventfd(0, os.EFD_NONBLOCK), or os.pipe() and os.set_blocking(write_end, False)), pass its descriptor to the function, and loop.add_reader(fd, on_ready) with a callback that reads the object and polls with the timeout 0.

A Windows host creates an auto-reset event with CreateEventW(NULL, FALSE, FALSE, NULL), passes it as (int64_t)(intptr_t)handle with DXFG_V4_READY_SIGNAL_WINDOWS_EVENT, and waits for it on a thread of the pool with RegisterWaitForSingleObject(), whose callback posts a message to the loop (or with WaitForSingleObject() or MsgWaitForMultipleObjects() in its own loop); the poll runs on the loop's thread. After the loop sees DXFG_EVENT_QUEUE_CLOSED, it calls UnregisterWaitEx() and then CloseHandle().

The functions are functions of the SDK: there are no QD methods that they call.

Attaches the subscription to the shared queue: its events go to the polls of the queue with the tag.

Attach it before the symbols are added to a subscription attached to a feed (QD refuses a listener then); a detached subscription may already have symbols, but then add its event listeners before attaching it. A closed subscription, or a subscription attached to a closed queue, is attached as left: its events are discarded and counted as dropped.

◆ dxfg_v4_EventQueue_close()

int32_t dxfg_v4_EventQueue_close ( graal_isolatethread_t * thread,
dxfg_v4_event_queue_t * queue )

Closes the queue: the poll in progress returns the events it has already retrieved or DXFG_EVENT_QUEUE_CLOSED, every later poll returns DXFG_EVENT_QUEUE_CLOSED. The events that no poll has returned are discarded and counted as dropped, and so are the events of the subscriptions that arrive later. The pending work of the subscriptions, the models and the collector runs on the calling thread without calling any listener of the application, and their later work runs at once on the posting thread the same way. The function does not wait for the polls in progress. Closing a closed queue does nothing (if the queue had a ready signal, the call returns once the SDK no longer writes to its object).

Releasing the last handle of the queue and of its executor (dxfg_v4_JavaObjectHandler_release()) closes it as well.

Parameters
[in]threadThe current GraalVM Isolate's thread.
[in]queueThe queue.
Returns
DXFG_EXECUTE_SUCCESSFULLY (0) on successful function execution or DXFG_EXECUTE_FAIL (-1) on error. Use dxfg_v4_get_and_clear_thread_exception() to determine if an exception was thrown.

◆ dxfg_v4_EventQueue_getDroppedEvents()

int32_t dxfg_v4_EventQueue_getDroppedEvents ( graal_isolatethread_t * thread,
dxfg_v4_event_queue_t * queue,
DXFG_OUT int64_t * droppedEvents )

Returns the number of the events of the attached subscriptions that were lost: the events that the SDK dropped as of the last retrieval (see DXFeedSubscription.getTotalDroppedEvents(); the last number for a closed subscription), plus the events that the queue dropped itself (a batch that failed to convert, the events of a closed queue or of a closed subscription). The number never decreases.

If the number grew since the previous poll, events were lost before the events that the last poll returned. For the indexed and time-series events (e.g. Order, Candle) the state built from the earlier events is then incomplete: subscribe again, or wait for the next snapshot. The number is one for the whole queue: to tell which subscription lost events, compare dxfg_v4_DXFeedSubscription_getTotalDroppedEvents() of each.

int32_t dxfg_v4_EventQueue_poll ( graal_isolatethread_t * thread,
dxfg_v4_event_queue_t * queue,
int64_t timeoutInMilliseconds,
int32_t limit,
DXFG_OUT dxfg_v4_event_type_list ** events )

Waits for the events of the attached subscriptions and returns them, without their tags (see dxfg_v4_EventQueue_pollTagged()).

The SDK retrieves the events on the calling thread and calls the event listeners of the subscriptions, and the listeners of the models and the collector on the queue, on it. After the first events, or after the first work of the models, the poll only completes the work that is already pending, so a steady flow does not keep it from returning. A batch may exceed the limit: the limit stops the retrieval, it does not split it. The limit counts the events only. The events of one subscription keep their order.

Compare dxfg_v4_EventQueue_getDroppedEvents() after each poll to detect a gap before the returned events.

If the events cannot be converted to the C structures, or an event listener of the subscription fails during the poll, the function fails, the events it has retrieved are dropped and counted, and the next poll continues with the next events.

Parameters
[in]threadThe current GraalVM Isolate's thread.
[in]queueThe queue.
[in]timeoutInMillisecondsHow long to wait for events: 0 returns at once; a negative value, or one above INT64_MAX / 1000000 (about 292 years), waits until events arrive, the queue is woken up or closed.
[in]limitThe number of events after which no more events are retrieved, 0 for no limit; must not be negative.
[out]eventsThe pointer to the variable that receives the list of events (release it with dxfg_v4_event_type_list_release()), or NULL if the result is not 0; must not be NULL.
Returns
DXFG_EXECUTE_SUCCESSFULLY (0) with a non-empty list, DXFG_EVENT_QUEUE_NO_EVENTS (1) if no events arrived before the timeout or the queue was woken up, or only the work of the models ran, DXFG_EVENT_QUEUE_CLOSED (2) if the queue is closed, or DXFG_EXECUTE_FAIL (-1) on error (also if called from a listener during a poll of the same queue, or if the waiting thread is interrupted). Use dxfg_v4_get_and_clear_thread_exception() to determine if an exception was thrown.

◆ dxfg_v4_EventQueue_setReadySignal()

int32_t dxfg_v4_EventQueue_setReadySignal ( graal_isolatethread_t * thread,
dxfg_v4_event_queue_t * queue,
dxfg_v4_ready_signal_kind_t kind,
int64_t handle )

Sets, replaces or removes the ready signal of the queue: the OS object that the SDK makes ready whenever the queue has work, so that an event loop can poll with the timeout 0 on its own thread (see the group description for the contract of the host).

The host creates the object, owns it and closes it: the SDK never creates, duplicates or closes it. The queue signals when it gets work while it is armed (the events of a subscription, the work of a model or the collector, a wake-up, the close), and the signal disarms it; each poll arms it again and signals at once if work remains. If work is pending when the signal is set, it is signalled before the function returns. A signal without work is possible: its poll returns DXFG_EVENT_QUEUE_NO_EVENTS. A queue has one signal: a new one replaces the old one, and the SDK does not write to the old object after the function returns. DXFG_V4_READY_SIGNAL_NONE removes the signal; the close of the queue signals once more and then removes it. A signal set on a closed queue is written once, before the function returns, and not kept.

The write to a file descriptor is of 8 bytes and never blocks: the descriptor must be non-blocking (O_NONBLOCK; EFD_NONBLOCK for an eventfd) and open for writing (the write end of a pipe, not its read end), which the function checks with fcntl(F_GETFL). A failed write or SetEvent() is logged once per signal and does not fail anything else.

Reset the object before each poll, as the group description says; for example, with epoll on Linux:

int fd = eventfd(0, EFD_NONBLOCK);
dxfg_v4_EventQueue_setReadySignal(thread, queue, DXFG_V4_READY_SIGNAL_FD, fd);
// ... wait for fd to be readable with epoll_wait(); then:
uint64_t counter;
read(fd, &counter, sizeof counter);
dxfg_v4_EventQueue_poll(thread, queue, 0, 0, &events);

Python asyncio loop.add_reader() and RegisterWaitForSingleObject() on Windows wait for the same objects (see the group description); the asyncio proactor loop and libuv on Windows cannot wait for them, so there the host keeps a thread that polls.

Parameters
[in]threadThe current GraalVM Isolate's thread.
[in]queueThe queue.
[in]kindThe kind of the object: DXFG_V4_READY_SIGNAL_FD on Linux, macOS and iOS, DXFG_V4_READY_SIGNAL_WINDOWS_EVENT on Windows, or DXFG_V4_READY_SIGNAL_NONE to remove the signal.
[in]handleThe file descriptor, or (int64_t)(intptr_t)HANDLE of the event; ignored for DXFG_V4_READY_SIGNAL_NONE.
Returns
DXFG_EXECUTE_SUCCESSFULLY (0) on successful function execution or DXFG_EXECUTE_FAIL (-1) on error (IllegalArgumentException for an unknown kind, a kind of the other OS, a descriptor outside 0 to INT32_MAX, a descriptor that is closed, blocking or read-only, and a handle 0 or -1; the message says how to fix it; the signal of the queue stays as it was). Use dxfg_v4_get_and_clear_thread_exception() to determine if an exception was thrown.
Experimental
It may change or be removed in a minor release, see The v4 functions.

3.13.0
