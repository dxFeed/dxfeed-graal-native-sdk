Detailed Description

The I/O statistics of a message connector (dxfg_v4_MessageConnector_getIoStatistics()): the ten cumulative I/O counters of QD's statistics and the open and closed connections.

The counters cover all the connections of the connector since it was created, the closed ones included, and are never reset: a reader takes the differences between two calls. Units: records, bytes, and microseconds for the lags and the round-trip times. The layout of the struct does not change: new counters will come with a new function.

The lags and the round-trip times are sums over the records, not samples: for each record QD adds the current value of its connection. Divide a sum by the matching record count for an average per record:

  • read_data_lag_sum_us / read_data_records is the average lag of the data received: the lag that the peer reported in its last heartbeat (how long the data waited in the peer and upstream of it) plus the round-trip time of the connection. It means something on the side that receives data (a feed); on a publisher it stays 0.
  • written_data_lag_sum_us / written_data_records is the average time a record spent in this process: from its arrival on another connection to the end of the composing of its message. The records that the application publishes itself have no arrival time and count the composing time only, so on a publisher the value is close to 0 and does not measure a delivery delay.
  • read_rtt_sum_us / (read_data_records + read_sub_records) and written_rtt_sum_us / (written_data_records + written_sub_records) are the round-trip time of the connections, weighted by their records.

An interval without records has no average: check that the record difference is not 0 before dividing.

QD measures the round-trip time with its heartbeats: it is 0 until the first heartbeats have gone both ways (about a second after the connect, the first heartbeat is sent after 0.5 s), so the records of the first moments add 0 to the round-trip sums and to the read lag sum. The time marks are microseconds of a 30-bit clock that wraps every 2^30 us (about 17.9 minutes), and each lag or round-trip time is a signed difference of two marks: a value of more than about 537 s comes out wrong, and a sum may then even decrease.

On iOS and the iOS Simulator the ten I/O counters stay 0: the SDK of these platforms has no QD monitoring (qds-monitoring), which keeps the statistics they come from. The connection counts work there too.

A connector stopped by a connect to another address, by dxfg_v4_DXEndpoint_disconnect() or by the close of its endpoint reads 0 for the ten I/O counters: QD closes its statistics and adds them to those of the endpoint. The connection counts stay. Read the statistics before such a stop to keep the last values; dxfg_v4_DXEndpoint_reconnect() and the drops of the connections do not stop the connector and keep the counters.

The bytes are those of the QTP messages as QD's statistics count them, heartbeats included; they may differ from the socket bytes of dxfg_v4_message_connector_stats_t (read_bytes, written_bytes), e.g. with TLS.
